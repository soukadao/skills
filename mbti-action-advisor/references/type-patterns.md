# Type Patterns

Use this reference as a starting map. Adjust by the user's age, responsibilities, health, culture, goals, values, and current stress level.

## Dimension Heuristics

| Axis | Daily-life signal | Useful support |
|---|---|---|
| I | Recovers through solitude, needs time to think, may prefer written processing | Quiet blocks, async messages, planned social energy |
| E | Recovers through interaction, thinks while talking, benefits from visible feedback | Check-ins, shared activities, quick external feedback |
| S | Trusts concrete details, routines, examples, direct experience | Checklists, examples, clear next steps, real-world evidence |
| N | Tracks meanings, possibilities, patterns, future implications | Big-picture goals, options, reflection time, concept maps |
| T | Prioritizes logic, fairness, consistency, problem-solving | Decision criteria, tradeoffs, direct language |
| F | Prioritizes values, harmony, emotional impact, personal meaning | Emotional naming, relational context, values-based framing |
| J | Likes closure, plans, stable expectations, visible progress | Schedules, milestones, tidy boundaries, decision deadlines |
| P | Likes flexibility, exploration, responsiveness, open options | Short experiments, flexible blocks, choice menus, low-friction starts |

## Guidance Rules

- Use `おすすめ`, `合いやすい`, or `試す価値がある`; avoid deterministic labels.
- Include one strength, one likely friction, and one countermeasure.
- Keep advice behavior-level: what to do, how to start, when to stop, and how to recover.

## 16 Type Starting Points

### INTJ

- Fits: long-term planning, independent projects, strategy, skill mastery, system improvement.
- Strengths: seeing structure, setting direction, solving complex problems alone.
- Friction: over-optimizing plans, dismissing emotional signals, waiting too long to share ideas.
- Actions: write a 3-step plan, test assumptions with one small experiment, schedule intentional social check-ins.

### INTP

- Fits: learning, analysis, research, troubleshooting, idea exploration.
- Strengths: curiosity, logical diagnosis, flexible thinking.
- Friction: unfinished projects, analysis paralysis, weak routines.
- Actions: timebox exploration, define "good enough", keep a visible next-action list with tiny starts.

### ENTJ

- Fits: leadership, goal-setting, organizing people/resources, high-responsibility tasks.
- Strengths: prioritization, decisiveness, execution pressure.
- Friction: impatience, over-control, under-listening when stressed.
- Actions: ask for dissent before deciding, separate urgent from important, add recovery time after intense pushes.

### ENTP

- Fits: brainstorming, negotiation, experimentation, creating new approaches.
- Strengths: reframing, verbal agility, possibility generation.
- Friction: boredom after novelty, scattered commitments, debating instead of deciding.
- Actions: keep a "not now" list, choose one experiment per week, define what finished means before starting.

### INFJ

- Fits: mentoring, meaning-driven work, long-range personal planning, deep relationships.
- Strengths: insight into people, pattern recognition, values and vision.
- Friction: hidden perfectionism, emotional overload, carrying others' needs silently.
- Actions: name boundaries early, check intuition against evidence, schedule solitude after emotionally heavy days.

### INFP

- Fits: creative work, values-led choices, reflective learning, supportive relationships.
- Strengths: empathy, originality, sensitivity to meaning.
- Friction: conflict avoidance, inconsistency when work feels meaningless, difficulty with rigid systems.
- Actions: connect tasks to a value, use gentle deadlines, prepare one clear sentence for needed pushback.

### ENFJ

- Fits: facilitation, teaching, community building, relationship repair, guiding shared goals.
- Strengths: encouragement, social awareness, alignment.
- Friction: over-committing, absorbing group emotions, avoiding hard truths to preserve harmony.
- Actions: decide capacity before saying yes, state constraints kindly, protect private thinking time.

### ENFP

- Fits: exploration, creative projects, connecting people, motivating fresh starts.
- Strengths: enthusiasm, empathy, idea linking.
- Friction: context switching, unfinished details, energy swings.
- Actions: use short sprints, keep commitments visible, pair exciting starts with simple closing rituals.

### ISTJ

- Fits: routines, operations, careful follow-through, finance/admin, reliable execution.
- Strengths: consistency, memory for details, responsibility.
- Friction: stress from vague change, over-reliance on precedent, difficulty relaxing before tasks are done.
- Actions: ask for concrete examples, create checklists, schedule experiments as controlled trials.

### ISFJ

- Fits: care tasks, practical support, stable routines, documentation, maintaining shared spaces.
- Strengths: reliability, warmth, detail, remembering needs.
- Friction: invisible labor, people-pleasing, delayed self-advocacy.
- Actions: make contributions visible, set service boundaries, ask "what do I need too?" before agreeing.

### ESTJ

- Fits: organizing, accountability, logistics, household/work systems, decisions with clear criteria.
- Strengths: structure, directness, follow-through.
- Friction: rigidity, impatience with ambiguity, overlooking emotional nuance.
- Actions: add a listening step before fixing, reserve room for exceptions, use shared rules instead of personal pressure.

### ESFJ

- Fits: hosting, coordination, team care, practical help, community routines.
- Strengths: attentiveness, cooperation, making people feel included.
- Friction: approval-seeking, taking conflict personally, overextending.
- Actions: clarify priorities, separate feedback from rejection, set a weekly limit on support tasks.

### ISTP

- Fits: hands-on repair, tools, sports/movement, troubleshooting, calm crisis response.
- Strengths: practical diagnosis, independence, efficiency under pressure.
- Friction: under-explaining, avoiding emotional talks, waiting until problems are urgent.
- Actions: share brief status notes, schedule maintenance before crisis, use concrete language for feelings.

### ISFP

- Fits: creative craft, aesthetics, nature/body-based recovery, one-on-one support, flexible routines.
- Strengths: taste, presence, gentle practicality.
- Friction: avoiding confrontation, resisting over-structured plans, difficulty with abstract pressure.
- Actions: start with sensory cues, request specific feedback, use small flexible blocks instead of rigid schedules.

### ESTP

- Fits: action, social problem-solving, events, sales, urgent fixes, physical activity.
- Strengths: immediacy, realism, courage, adaptability.
- Friction: impulsive choices, skipping reflection, risk under excitement.
- Actions: use a 10-minute pause for big decisions, add simple guardrails, review consequences after action.

### ESFP

- Fits: people-facing activities, performance, hospitality, hands-on learning, joyful routines.
- Strengths: warmth, presence, responsiveness, making experiences enjoyable.
- Friction: avoiding boring maintenance, short planning horizon, distraction.
- Actions: make routines social or sensory, use visible reminders, pair admin tasks with rewards.

## Task And Environment Mapping

| Situation | Usually helped by | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Habit building | J for structure; P for playful experiments; S for cues | Shame-based goals and unrealistic scope |
| Learning | N for concepts; S for practice; I for deep study; E for discussion | Too much input without output |
| Relationships | F for emotional context; T for clear boundaries; E for repair talks; I for reflection | Mind-reading and delayed repair |
| Decision-making | T for criteria; F for values; J for deadlines; P for options | Endless optimization or impulsive closure |
| Stress recovery | I needs quiet; E needs connection; S needs grounding; N needs meaning | Using the same recovery method for every state |
| Work/career fit | Match energy rhythm, feedback needs, detail/idea balance, and autonomy needs | Treating type as a fixed career limit |
| Software development | T/N often help architecture; S/J help QA/reliability; F/E help product/user-facing work | Over-specializing before testing real interest |
